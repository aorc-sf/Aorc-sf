PUBLIC INTRODUCTION

Economic freedom requires ownership.

For generations, descendants of American slavery were systematically restricted from accumulating many of the assets that create lasting economic power. The Resource Center is being built to develop, explain, organize around, and eventually help implement an ownership-centered reparative strategy.

Homes

Expand housing supply, homeownership pathways, community ownership, rehabilitation and activation of underused housing.

Businesses

Increase startup capital, acquisitions, commercial ownership, technical assistance, procurement and job creation.

Investments

Create permanent trust capital and individual productive assets capable of compounding across generations.

FLAGSHIP POLICY FRAMEWORK

The American Ownership & Reparative Capital Plan

The plan begins with a specific obligation: repairing the economic injury created by American chattel slavery and the government-sanctioned exclusion that followed it. It is designed around ownership rather than permanent dependency.

Restitution through ownership

Housing, investment capital, business capitalization, education, trust capital, recurring benefits and documented family-specific restitution.

Permanent productive capital

Invest part of the capitalization in an independently governed permanent trust rather than spending every dollar once.

Build more housing

Pair reparative purchasing power with construction, rehabilitation, community ownership and vacancy activation.

Create more businesses

Turn reparative capital into employers, suppliers, commercial property, tax revenue and productive enterprise.

Fiscal discipline

Use progressive financing, institutional restitution, limited borrowing, verified savings, investment returns and sunset mechanisms.

Economic autonomy

The ultimate goal is household power: enough capital to make meaningful choices, survive shocks, invest, build and pass assets forward.

CANONICAL NATIONAL POLICY

Complete 14-part plan

A National Program for Restitution, Ownership, Housing, Growth and Economic Independence.

I. Restitution Through Ownership

Where public policy systematically prevented people from accumulating capital, an appropriate remedy should restore access to capital.

  • individual investment capital
  • housing and homeownership capital
  • business capitalization
  • education capital
  • a permanent beneficiary trust
  • recurring public-service benefits
  • documented family-specific restitution where identifiable losses can be established

The objective is not permanent dependency on government. The objective is greater independence from it.

II. A Permanent Reparations Trust

A portion of national reparative capitalization would be placed into an independently managed permanent trust owning diversified productive assets capable of generating returns for future generations.

Under the current model, approximately $2 trillion of the broader capitalization is committed to permanent trust capital, with professional management, beneficiary protections, public audits, investment diversification and a stabilization reserve.

III. Benefits Without Permanent Dependency

Recurring Descendant Benefits would emphasize high-leverage services such as public transportation, mobility, licensing assistance, business and financial technical assistance, and digital public services.

Large capital benefits such as education, homeownership and business capitalization would use dedicated capital accounts and milestone programs rather than unlimited annual entitlements.

IV. Build More Housing for America

Reparations should not simply create more purchasing power for the same limited number of homes. The plan pairs capital with new multifamily construction, rehabilitation, mixed-income development, community land trusts, shared-equity ownership, enabling infrastructure and conversion of underused properties.

V. Put Empty Housing Back to Work

Cities facing severe affordability problems should be able to develop legally sustainable housing-activation policies for units deliberately left vacant for extended periods. The principle is: use the housing or help finance more housing.

VI. Create More American Businesses

One of the most productive uses of reparative capital can be business formation.

Capital → Business Formation → Employment → Income → Consumption → Tax Revenue → Additional Investment

Business ownership gives reparative capital the potential to become productive national capital.

VII. Strengthen the American Consumer

Households with stronger balance sheets can purchase goods and services, withstand shocks and make longer-term investments. The objective is increasing the number of Americans capable of owning, investing, producing and purchasing.

VIII. Improve America’s Fiscal Position

The plan combines progressive financing, evidence-based institutional restitution, limited borrowing, permanent invested capital, government-efficiency savings, improper-payment reduction, voluntary contributions, sunset provisions and investment returns.

The objective is to create assets while controlling liabilities.

IX. Reward Government Efficiency

Independently verified federal savings from improper-payment reduction, duplication, unnecessary overhead, obsolete spending, inefficient procurement and legally available unused appropriations should reduce borrowing. Savings count only after they become real and measurable.

X. Voluntary National Participation

Americans, companies, philanthropies, universities, religious institutions and other organizations should be able to contribute voluntarily to the trust or associated programs. Voluntary contributions supplement rather than replace government obligations.

XI. National Reconciliation

Voluntary counseling, historical education, community dialogue and evidence-based prejudice-reduction programs can help people confront the social consequences of racism without assigning inherited guilt or treating races as enemies.

Reconciliation should accompany restitution. Neither should substitute for the other.

XII. What Does the Average American Gain?
  • More housing
  • More employment
  • More customers
  • More businesses
  • Stronger neighborhoods
  • Greater government efficiency
  • Less reliance on perpetual deficit spending
  • A larger productive economy
  • A country with more owners
XIII. A Different Definition of Economic Freedom

Economic freedom should mean more than choosing between employers or deciding how to spend a paycheck. It should mean possessing enough capital to make meaningful choices: leave a bad job, survive a loss of income, relocate, educate yourself, start something, invest, help family and take risks.

XIV. The National Proposition

America can address a legitimate reparative obligation while simultaneously investing in its own future.

  • Repair what was taken.
  • Create what was denied.
  • Build more housing.
  • Create more businesses.
  • Increase ownership.
  • Reduce waste.
  • Protect taxpayers from unnecessary debt.
  • Create permanent capital instead of permanent dependency.
  • Give ordinary Americans greater economic autonomy.

This is reparative justice built around ownership — and an investment in a more productive, financially independent America.

PUBLIC EDUCATION

Frequently asked questions

What is the Resource Center?

A proposed public education, research, organizing and implementation institution. Its flagship national policy framework is the American Ownership & Reparative Capital Plan.

Who is the descendant-based framework intended for?

Principally descendants of persons enslaved in the United States, using evidence, administrative standards and due process. Final eligibility rules would require formal legal and program design.

Is this just a cash-payment plan?

No. Direct cash can have a role, but the central strategy is capital formation through homes, businesses, investments, education, land, productive assets and permanent trust capital.

Why does the plan emphasize housing supply?

Because giving people more purchasing power without adding housing can simply intensify competition for scarce homes. The plan pairs reparative purchasing power with construction and rehabilitation.

Does the plan assign inherited guilt?

No. The case is based on historical evidence, institutional responsibility, measurable economic injury and repair through expanded future ownership.
