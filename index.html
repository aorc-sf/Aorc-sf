EDUCATION & ORGANIZING

Knowledge becomes power when people can act together.

AORC's education work is not just about explaining policy. It is about building the civic infrastructure needed for descendants and supporters to understand what government is doing, identify leverage points, communicate priorities, and act together instead of approaching institutions one person at a time.

1. Build the information infrastructure

Translate legislation, budgets, hearings, programs, contracts, and implementation decisions into clear electronic updates people can actually understand and share.

2. Build the community network

Use opt-in signups, surveys, opportunity alerts, and public education to develop an organized network with shared information and measurable priorities.

3. Turn numbers into representation

Use participation data, survey results, testimony, letters, meetings, and public comment to show agencies and elected officials that the community is organized around specific outcomes.

4. Track the money and implementation

Follow appropriations, agency actions, contracts, grants, and program results so public announcements can be compared with what actually reaches communities.

5. Create leadership pathways

Give volunteers and future staff clear roles in outreach, research, education, organizing, partnerships, and local representation as AORC grows.

6. Convert advocacy into institutions

The long-term objective is durable community capacity: resources, services, contracts, ownership pathways, and institutions that remain useful beyond a single campaign.

AORC organizing model: Inform → Connect → Measure → Mobilize → Negotiate → Build.

FLAGSHIP POLICY FRAMEWORK

The American Ownership & Reparative Capital Plan

AORC's broader policy work remains centered on repairing historical economic exclusion through ownership, productive assets, business formation, housing, investment, education, and long-term economic independence.

Restitution through ownership

Use reparative policy to expand access to homes, businesses, investment capital, education, and durable productive assets.

Build more housing

Pair purchasing power with construction, rehabilitation, community ownership, and activation of underused housing.

Create more businesses

Turn capital into employers, suppliers, commercial ownership, jobs, tax revenue, and stronger local economies.

Permanent capital

Explore independently governed trust structures designed to preserve and compound productive capital across generations.

Fiscal discipline

Evaluate progressive financing, institutional restitution, verified savings, investment returns, and safeguards against unnecessary debt.

Economic autonomy

The objective is greater household power: the ability to survive shocks, make choices, invest, build, and pass assets forward.

Policy-model guardrail: AORC policy scenarios are proposals unless specifically identified as enacted law or funded public programs. They are not guaranteed benefits or official government estimates.

Petition statement

We call on public agencies, philanthropic institutions, universities, foundations, businesses, and community partners to support the creation of the American Ownership Resource Center as a public-interest hub for education, resource navigation, community organizing, reparative economic development, housing and business ownership strategy, descendant-focused policy work, and implementation accountability.

We support a proposed first-stage startup funding goal of $250,000–$500,000 to establish basic operating capacity, with funding subject to transparent budgeting, conflict-of-interest safeguards, public reporting, and measurable deliverables.
